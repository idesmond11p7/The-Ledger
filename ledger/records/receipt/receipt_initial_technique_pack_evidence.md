# Craft Receipt — Initial Technique Pack Evidence & Source Audit

**Event type:** observation and research synthesis  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-26  
**Status:** reported; initial draft, not user-approved methodology.

## Work performed

Read the eight chapter records currently present in the Ledger repository and reviewed the existing chapter craft/chronology receipts. Researched focalization, free indirect discourse, narrative gaps, montage, narrative inference, reading prediction, prose rhythm, poetry/prose reading behavior, and typography using the linked publisher pages and abstracts in the two Research Receipts.

## Source-grounded observations

- Chapter 1's third-person limited focalization restricts visual certainty while remembered room geometry preserves orientation.
- Chapter 2's first-person narration shifts the access pattern; the POV difference from Chapter 1 is noted but not resolved here.
- Chapter 3 repeats “tomorrow” and fragments the collision through sound, sensory narrowing, and memory intrusion.
- Chapter 4 uses light and spatial relations to render cosmic scale, then returns to grass, air, animals, road, and headlights. The cosmic sequence remains Jonah's perception.
- Chapter 5 turns reincarnation into physical and logistical problems through body detail, documents, maps, and transport.
- Chapter 6 makes embodiment and institutional hierarchy perceptible through routine, sensory specificity, route correction, and an uncertain shadow clue.
- Chapter 7 uses procedural repetition and small sounds to control exam rhythm; its final sentence is repeated at Chapter 8's opening.
- Chapter 8 uses the dossier's mismatch with lived memory and a failed social prediction to examine the limits of “Think ahead.”

## Derived technique directions

The initial pack set covers: light as a causal perceptual event; focalization and selective attention; motifs whose meaning changes; productive gaps and ellipsis; stillness and timing; meaning generated by juxtaposition; prose cadence; typography; reader prediction and inference; kinetic syntax; and lyricism/refrain.

These names are working handles. Not every label is an established scholarly term. The pack procedures are interpretations and craft hypotheses, not universal laws.
