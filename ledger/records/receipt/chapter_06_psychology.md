- Ira's adaptation is practical, not complete. He can prepare, travel, eat, and correct mistakes while still experiencing body estrangement and embarrassment.
- He nearly boards the wrong train but checks the timetable, catches the error, and calmly waits for the Local. The narrative values correcting a mistake over freezing or expecting rescue.
- The pork bun provides sensory grounding and briefly counters the fear that he is a ghost or no longer materially alive.
- Being mistaken for a girl embarrasses him and changes how he imagines others perceive him. One commuter's kindness does not prove everyone sees him the same way.
- At the academy, he notices institutional hierarchy and the unnamed woman's abnormal shadow, but prioritizes the exam rather than investigating.
- “Fine, I thought. Then they're mine now” marks provisional ownership of the new hands, not full emotional resolution.
