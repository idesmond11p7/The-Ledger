# Chapter 9 Receipt: Psychology, Scene Logic, and World Rendering

**Status:** Working receipt v1  
**Primary sources:** Ready Chapter 8 and its psychology receipt; Chapter 9 event outline; Ledger experiential narration philosophy and technique packs.

## Ira's established psychological position

- Ira wants ordinary contact but is not socially fluent.
- He can prepare and plan, but his predictions can be wrong.
- Chapter 8 shows him correctly anticipating one question and then answering a different question from the one actually asked.
- His new rule, “Think ahead. Pay attention. Stop walking into things unprepared,” is a coping strategy with a latent self-blame trap.
- He has not mastered uncertainty, social interpretation, or the life attached to the name Ira Lowe.
- The Chapter 9 outline states that Ira's present nervous system is less conditioned to horror than Jonah's old one. Treat this as a difference in tolerance, not a deficit of intelligence.

## Chapter 9 psychological test

The chapter should test the rule rather than reward it with perfect prediction.

1. Ira prepares a route and makes at least one meaningful navigation correction. His improvement is practical and earned.
2. He misreads at least one ordinary social cue. Do not repeat Chapter 8's exact conversational mistake.
3. The errand offers usefulness and ordinary inclusion. Ira's motivation is mixed: curiosity, practical purpose, and a desire to be included.
4. When the cat bolts, Ira follows because a four-year-old child is distressed, the cat is close, and he believes he can retrieve it. Do not turn him into an unprompted hero.
5. Once inside the forest, his attention narrows around finding the cat. This provides a plausible reason he misses early signs of the boundary crossing.
6. Fear develops through evidence and bodily response. He does not immediately identify a supernatural threat.
7. He remains capable of small practical decisions even as his explanatory model fails.
8. The final reflection creates shock without giving him knowledge of the entities or the realm.

## Cat incident requirements

- The child is visibly about four years old and cannot safely follow the cat alone.
- The responsible adult reacts; the event must not depend on negligent guardianship.
- Ira is the nearest older person with a clear route to the cat.
- The cat remains intermittently trackable through plausible evidence.
- Ira follows for an immediate, concrete reason rather than abstract bravery.

## City traversal requirements

- The city is the chapter's main attraction before the horror pivot.
- Build a mental map through paths, edges, nodes, landmarks, and route decisions.
- Reuse only the geography actually established in Chapters 6–8 as established anchors.
- Treat hospital, red-light district, bus route, forest, and errand destination as proposed chapter geography until mapped.
- Side incidents must alter route, attention, pace, interpretation, or social understanding. Do not add random cameos.
- Adult nightlife remains adult social context. Ira is sixteen and must not be sexualized or written as a voyeur.

## Scene-construction requirements

Use the Ledger's experiential narration philosophy:
- distinguish seeing, noticing, interpreting, and knowing;
- preserve viewpoint access and uncertainty;
- render the environment as a changing causal system, not a list of sensory details;
- give sounds sources, positions, material paths, and changing relationships;
- keep action physically reconstructable.

For kinetic events, use the diagnostic chain:
**Initiation → Vector → Resistance → Contact → Consequence → Attention shift.**

The forest transition should be gradual and retrospectively legible. The house must be materially coherent. The floor collapse must have a physical cause. The tooth necklace catches on Ira's clothing accidentally and remains unnoticed.

## Cutscene distinction

There are two different cinematic sequences:
- **Errand cutscene:** bounded heteroperspective, human/social information, no lore dump.
- **Void cutscene:** supernatural visual and acoustic destabilization, no definitive explanation of the phenomenon.

Do not make them stylistically identical. The first clarifies a social event; the second destabilizes the reader's model of space.

## Failure conditions

- City becomes a travel montage.
- Ira becomes socially omniscient after Chapter 8.
- The errand is a generic fetch quest with an arbitrary countdown.
- The cat event relies on an implausibly absent adult.
- The forest becomes generically dark without changing spatial evidence.
- Entities are shown too clearly or given explanatory dialogue.
- The house is a prop rather than a place.
- The floor fall is physically impossible to reconstruct.
- The necklace is treated as a chosen artifact or consciously discovered.
- The final image is followed by an explanatory paragraph that weakens the cliffhanger.
