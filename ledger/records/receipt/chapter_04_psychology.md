# Chapter 4 Analysis Receipt: Jonah's Psychological State

**Source:** [Chapter 4 — Beyond](https://github.com/idesmond11p7/The-Ledger/blob/main/ledger/records/chapter/chapter_04_beyond.md)

- The absence of bodily sensation destabilizes Jonah's basic sense of existence. Fear persists even when he cannot identify a body or location.
- Cosmic scale does not erase personal attachment: the thought of his mother, Daniel, Earth, and ordinary life gives the experience emotional stakes.
- When he returns to the roadside, familiar sensory details are grounding but also make the ordinary world feel cramped after the perceived cosmic vastness.
- The new body's efficient balance and automatic scanning of routes and cover feel unfamiliar but usable. Their cause is unresolved; do not assign a power or training history yet.
- The mirror transforms the earlier self-esteem motif into a concrete identity crisis.
- Jonah's final profanity is a compressed reaction to shock, not evidence that he has accepted the situation or recovered emotionally.
