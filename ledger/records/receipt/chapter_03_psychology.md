# Chapter 3 Analysis Receipt: Jonah's Behavior and Relationships

**Source:** [Chapter 3 — Maybe Tomorrow](https://github.com/idesmond11p7/The-Ledger/blob/main/ledger/records/chapter/chapter_03_maybe_tomorrow.md)

- Jonah is increasingly aware of his cognitive distortions but cannot reliably interrupt them. “You don't know that” is progress, not immunity.
- The notebook exchange exposes a defensive reflex: he interprets concern as pity before checking the girl's intent, then recognizes the mistake. Her identity is not stated; do not equate her with Maya.
- His list converts diffuse anxiety about adulthood into manageable tasks. He wants consistency and control, not greatness or guaranteed destiny.
- The sunrise memory matters because it belongs to his mother's view of him before insecurity; he values being remembered as a child she knew.
- Daniel is a reciprocal friend and source of ordinary joy, not merely a plot device to be abandoned.
- The fatal chase grows from an understandable reaction to phone theft. Do not moralize the accident as punishment for procrastination or phone use.
