Chapter 3 — Maybe Tomorrow
I woke up before my alarm.

Again.

For a few seconds, I just lay there.

The ceiling was still the ceiling.

Same pale paint.

Same thin crack running from the corner toward the light fixture.

I had been meaning to fix that.

For months.

I reached for my phone.

6:11.

My alarm was set for 6:30.

Nineteen minutes.

I could go back to sleep.

I probably should.

Instead, I sat up.

For some reason, that felt like an accomplishment.

Not a big one.

Just enough to make me feel like I'd done something right.

I stood.

My feet hit the cold floor.

I walked to the mirror.

My hair was a mess.

My eyes looked tired.

I stared at myself.

"Ugly."

I waited.

"...Not ugly."

Another pause.

"Average."

I frowned.

Why did I keep doing that?

Maybe because average was such a strange word.

It wasn't an insult.

It wasn't a compliment.

It just meant normal.

And I hated how much I hated being normal.

I left the room.

Mom was already in the kitchen.

She looked over when I walked in.

"You woke up early."

"I guess."

She glanced at the clock.

"You have school in an hour."

"I know."

"So why are you standing there?"

I looked around the kitchen.

"...I don't know."

She shook her head.

There was a plate on the table.

I sat down.

She put a cup beside it.

"Eat."

"I was going to."

"You say that every morning."

"And every morning I eventually eat."

"After I tell you."

"Still counts."

She gave me a look.

I smiled.

She tried not to.

Didn't quite manage it.

For a moment, everything was normal.

Just breakfast.

Just Mom.

Just another morning.

Then she said:

"You know, you used to wake up at five."

I stopped.

"What?"

"When you were little."

"No, I didn't."

"You did."

"Why?"

She shrugged.

"You wanted to watch the sunrise."

I smiled.

"That's embarrassing."

"You were six."

"Still embarrassing."

"You used to drag your blanket into the living room and sit by the window."

I stared at her.

"I don't remember that."

"I do."

She went back to what she was doing.

I smiled again.

I liked that.

There were apparently versions of me that existed in other people's memories.

Versions I'd forgotten.

Six-year-old Jonah.

Sitting by a window before sunrise because he wanted to see something.

I wondered what happened to that kid.

Mom handed me my lunch.

"Don't forget this."

I looked at the bag.

"Oh."

She raised an eyebrow.

"Thanks."

"You're welcome, Jonah Laurence."

The full name.

That meant I was supposed to be paying attention.

I took the bag.

"See you later."

"Don't be late."

"I won't."

I left.

The morning was ordinary.

Cars.

People.

A dog barking behind a fence.

Someone arguing about a parking space.

A bus groaning its way down the road.

Nothing strange.

Nothing dramatic.

Nothing worth remembering.

I liked mornings like this.

Because nobody was paying attention to me.

And for once, I didn't mind.

Daniel was waiting near the corner.

The second he saw me, he raised one hand.

"Finally!"

"I'm early."

"You're four minutes late."

"Then you're four minutes early."

He stared at me.

"That's not how time works."

"It is if I'm the one who's late."

"That doesn't even make sense."

"Sounds like a you problem."

He laughed.

We started walking.

A few seconds passed.

Then he looked at me.

"So."

"So?"

"The assignment."

I looked at him.

He looked at me.

Neither of us said anything.

Then we both started laughing.

"You're useless," he said.

"You asked me knowing the answer."

"I was hoping you'd become disciplined overnight."

I shrugged.

"I woke up before my alarm."

Daniel stopped walking.

"What?"

"Yeah."

He looked genuinely concerned.

"Who are you?"

"Shut up."

"I'm serious."

"I just woke up early."

"That's exactly what a replacement would say."

I shoved his shoulder.

He laughed.

I laughed too.

And just like that, the morning became easier.

School was normal.

Mostly.

Teachers talked.

Students complained.

Pens disappeared.

Someone got yelled at for talking.

Daniel nearly made me laugh in class.

I nearly got caught.

Then break came.

We sat behind the science block where the concrete wall blocked most of the noise.

The sun had warmed the pavement.

Daniel was telling me a story.

A stupid story.

Which meant it was probably true.

"...and then she tells me I'm supposed to apologize."

"You probably did something."

"I didn't."

"You definitely did."

"I did not."

"What did you say?"

He hesitated.

"That's not important."

I laughed.

"Then you did something."

"Okay, technically—"

"Exactly."

He threw a bottle cap at me.

I caught it.

"You're annoying."

"You're the one who started it."

"That's debatable."

"No, it isn't."

We laughed again.

For a while, neither of us cared about anything.

Eventually the conversation drifted.

Money.

School.

The future.

Daniel wanted to be rich.

"How rich?"

"Rich."

"That's not a number."

"Enough."

"Enough for what?"

"Everything."

I shook my head.

"That's not a plan."

"Sure it is."

He looked at me.

"What about you?"

"What?"

"What do you want?"

I opened my mouth.

A joke came first.

I almost said it.

Instead, I shrugged.

"I don't know."

Daniel frowned.

"You're seventeen."

"So?"

"You're not supposed to know."

I smiled.

"Maybe."

He nudged my shoulder.

"You'll figure it out."

Simple.

He said it like it was obvious.

I didn't answer.

But I remembered it.

The cyberbullying started again after lunch.

I knew before I even opened my phone.

That little hesitation.

That instinctive feeling that something unpleasant was waiting behind the screen.

I opened the app.

One post.

Then another.

A screenshot of something I'd said weeks ago.

A caption.

A reply.

Another reply.

I scrolled.

More.

Some of it was stupid.

Some of it was exaggerated.

Some of it wasn't even true.

That should have made it easier.

It didn't.

I kept reading.

Then I read the comments again.

Why?

I didn't know.

Maybe I was looking for the point where it stopped hurting.

It didn't.

I locked my phone.

Put it face-down.

Looked at the board.

The teacher was talking.

I listened.

For maybe ten seconds.

My phone buzzed.

I ignored it.

Another buzz.

I looked at it.

Don't.

I picked it up.

More messages.

My face felt hot.

I locked it again.

Why do I care?

Because everyone else cared.

Or I thought they did.

That was the problem.

I didn't actually know what anyone thought.

I knew what I imagined they thought.

Sometimes those felt exactly the same.

I put my phone away.

They're right.

I stared at the board.

No, they're not.

A second passed.

Why do I care?

Because everyone else cares.

Do they?

Doesn't matter.

I looked around the classroom.

Nobody was looking at me.

At least...

I didn't think they were.

A guy two seats ahead laughed.

My brain immediately supplied an explanation.

He's laughing about you.

I caught myself.

You don't know that.

I watched him for another second.

He was talking to his friend.

Probably something completely unrelated.

I looked away.

That bothered me.

Not because I was necessarily wrong.

Because I had no idea whether I was right.

And I'd already decided.

After class, someone caught up with me.

"Jonah."

I turned.

She held out a notebook.

"You left this."

I took it.

"Oh."

"You were kind of quiet earlier."

"I'm fine."

She hesitated.

"You sure?"

Something inside me tightened.

I hated that question.

Not because it was bad.

Because I didn't know how to answer it.

"Yeah."

She looked at me.

I looked away.

Then I said:

"You don't have to do that."

"Do what?"

"Feel sorry for me."

She blinked.

"I wasn't."

"Whatever."

I walked away.

My steps felt too fast.

I made it around the corner.

Stopped.

Looked back.

She was still standing there.

She didn't look angry.

She looked confused.

Genuinely confused.

My stomach sank.

Oh.

I knew what I'd done.

She'd noticed I wasn't doing well.

That's it.

She hadn't insulted me.

She hadn't mocked me.

She hadn't even said anything particularly personal.

She'd just...

noticed.

And I'd decided what she meant before I actually knew.

Again.

I rubbed my face.

I'm an asshole.

Then another thought came.

No.

You're embarrassed.

I frowned.

That's not an excuse.

I stood there for a few seconds.

That was the annoying thing about self-awareness.

Sometimes you could watch yourself making a mistake in real time.

And still make it.

Maybe that was what discipline actually meant.

Not waking up early.

Not making schedules.

Not pretending you suddenly had your entire life under control.

Maybe it meant being able to stop yourself.

Think.

Then act.

I liked that idea.

A lot.

Because if that was true...

then maybe I could actually change.

By the time school ended, I felt better.

Not happy.

Not miserable.

Just...

bummed out.

There was a difference.

I could still laugh.

I could still talk.

I could still look forward to things.

I just had this annoying weight sitting somewhere behind my ribs.

Seventeen.

That number kept coming back.

Seventeen.

Still young.

But somehow already late.

Everyone kept saying I had time.

Of course I had time.

But how much of it had I already wasted?

Childhood was gone.

I couldn't get it back.

Years I'd spent doing stupid things.

Hours I'd spent scrolling.

Days I'd wasted.

Opportunities I'd ignored.

What if I'd started earlier?

What if I'd been more disciplined?

What if I'd actually taken myself seriously?

Maybe I'd be better by now.

Maybe I'd be smarter.

More confident.

More talented.

More—

I stopped myself.

That was the trap.

I didn't need to become some extraordinary person.

I just wanted to become better.

That should have been enough.

But there was a part of me that wanted more.

A part that hated the idea of being average.

I knew talent mattered.

Of course it did.

Some people were simply better at things.

Some people learned faster.

Some people were naturally attractive.

Some people were naturally confident.

Some people could walk into a room and own it without even trying.

That was life.

I understood that.

I just didn't like it.

Because if talent wasn't enough...

then what was?

Work.

Discipline.

Consistency.

Control.

Those words felt better.

They made the future feel less random.

I could make a plan.

Wake up earlier.

Exercise.

Study properly.

Read more.

Learn something useful.

Use my phone less.

Talk to people more.

Sleep properly.

Stop procrastinating.

Stop reacting.

Actually...

Maybe I could write all of it down.

A real schedule.

Something I'd actually follow.

I felt myself getting excited.

Not because I believed I'd become perfect.

Just because—

I could change.

That thought hit differently.

I could actually change.

Maybe I wasn't late.

Maybe I just hadn't started.

Then another thought slipped in.

If I control everything I can control...

I paused.

That sounded reasonable.

...then eventually things have to go right.

I knew that wasn't entirely true.

Obviously.

Life didn't work like that.

But it was close enough to something true that I wanted to believe it.

If I couldn't control other people, I could control myself.

If I couldn't control the past, I could control tomorrow.

If I couldn't control whether people liked me, I could control whether I respected myself.

That was enough.

It had to be.

I started walking again.

Tomorrow.

I'd start tomorrow.

Daniel caught up with me near the school gate.

"You going home?"

"Yeah."

"Same."

We walked together.

For a while, neither of us said anything.

Then Daniel looked at me.

"You know what?"

"What?"

"You're actually less annoying when you're not thinking so hard."

I laughed.

"That's your nicest insult yet."

"I'm serious."

"Thanks?"

He smiled.

Then he pointed at me.

"Don't forget the assignment."

"I won't."

"You will."

"I won't."

"You absolutely will."

"Watch me."

He started walking backward.

"I know you, Laurence."

I smiled.

"Go home."

He turned around.

I watched him disappear down the street.

For some reason, I stayed there.

Just for a moment.

Then I went home.

The evening was quiet.

I ate.

I did some homework.

Not much.

I spent too long on my phone.

Ironically.

Then I opened my notes app.

I typed:

Things I need to fix.

I stared at it.

Then started listing things.

Wake up earlier.

Exercise.

Study.

Read.

Less phone.

More social.

Better sleep.

Stop reacting emotionally.

Actually finish things.

Learn something useful.

Figure out what I want.

I stared at the list.

It was ridiculous.

It was ambitious.

It was probably unrealistic.

I smiled anyway.

Maybe that was okay.

Maybe I didn't have to know how everything would work.

Maybe I just had to start.

Tomorrow.

I put my phone down.

A few minutes later, it buzzed.

I picked it up.

Unknown number.

I opened the message.

You looked happy today.

My smile disappeared.

Another message appeared.

Enjoy it while you can.

I stared at the screen.

My chest tightened.

I blocked the number.

Then I sat there.

Waiting.

Nothing happened.

No new message.

No call.

Nothing.

I put the phone down.

Ignore it.

That was the sensible thing.

So I did.

For about thirty seconds.

Then I picked it up again.

Nothing.

I locked it.

Put it away.

I wasn't going to let some anonymous idiot ruin my evening.

I had plans.

Tomorrow.

I'd start tomorrow.

I went to bed later than I should have.

I stared at the ceiling.

The crack was still there.

Same as this morning.

Same as yesterday.

Same as—

I stopped.

The previous night.

The thing in my room.

The darkness.

The breathing.

The voice.

I remembered it.

My skin prickled.

Maybe it had been a nightmare.

Probably.

Stress.

Too much screen time.

Whatever.

My brain had probably just...

done something weird.

That happened.

I turned onto my side.

Tomorrow was Thursday.

School.

Daniel.

Maybe I'd apologize to that girl.

Maybe.

I'd start the schedule.

I'd actually stick to it this time.

I'd get better.

Not perfect.

Just better.

That was enough.

Wasn't it?

I closed my eyes.

And slept.

The next afternoon, I was going home.

I remember that.

I remember the sun.

I remember the pavement.

I remember thinking about the list on my phone.

I remember thinking I should probably add don't procrastinate to it.

Then someone slammed into my shoulder.

Hard.

"What the—"

My hand went empty.

I looked down.

Nothing.

I looked ahead.

The guy was already running.

My phone was in his hand.

For half a second, I just stood there.

Then my brain caught up.

"HEY!"

He kept running.

"HEY! GIVE THAT BACK!"

I took off.

My shoes hammered the pavement.

SMACK! SMACK! SMACK!

He was fast.

Not impossibly fast.

Just fast enough.

"STOP!"

He didn't.

I pushed harder.

My phone.

That was all I cared about.

My phone.

Everything was on that thing.

Pictures.

Messages.

Notes.

Accounts.

My stupid schedule.

The list.

Tomorrow.

I could see the blue case.

Mine.

I was gaining on him.

Maybe.

No.

Definitely.

"GIVE IT BACK!"

He looked over his shoulder.

That was when I knew I was catching him.

Five meters.

Maybe six.

My lungs burned.

My heart hammered against my ribs.

THUD. THUD. THUD.

I pushed harder.

Then—

VRRROOOOOOOOOOOOM—

Something roared somewhere beside me.

I barely noticed.

The thief was still ahead.

I kept running.

THUD. THUD. THUD.

My heartbeat.

My shoes.

My breath.

Everything narrowing.

Thief.

Phone.

Run.

Then rain touched my cheek.

One drop.

Tap.

Another.

Tap.

I glanced upward.

The sky had changed.

Clouds had swallowed the afternoon.

That was weird.

It had been clear.

Hadn't it?

Then—

SHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH.

Rain poured down.

"What the hell?!"

It didn't matter.

The thief was still ahead.

I ran.

The road seemed longer now.

Maybe it was the rain.

Maybe I'd taken a wrong turn.

I couldn't tell.

There.

The thief.

Still running.

Still carrying my phone.

"COME BACK!"

My voice cracked.

I pushed harder.

SMACK! SMACK! SMACK!

Then something enormous moved through the edge of my vision.

Black.

Tall.

Fast.

I didn't look.

I couldn't.

The thief was right there.

Eight meters.

Seven.

Six.

I reached.

"HEY!"

The engine behind me grew louder.

Not louder.

Closer.

VRRROOOOOOOOOOOOOOMMMMM—

The pavement trembled beneath my shoes.

My teeth clicked together.

I barely registered it.

The thief was getting away.

Five meters.

Four.

I stretched my hand out.

"GOT YOU—!"

A shadow swallowed the road.

The rain around me exploded sideways.

WHOOOOOOOOM!

Wind slammed into my body.

I stumbled.

"What—"

The sound behind me changed.

A metallic shriek.

A monstrous grinding roar.

KRRRRRRRRRRRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAAA—

I turned.

Just a little.

Just enough.

And finally saw it.

Black metal.

Rain.

Headlights.

A wall.

No.

Not a wall.

A vehicle.

Maybe.

My brain couldn't make sense of the size.

It towered over the street.

The thing seemed to stretch backward into the rain forever.

The road looked tiny beneath it.

Wrong.

Everything about it was wrong.

I stared.

My mouth opened.

"What the fuck—?!"

The headlights hit me.

WHITE.

My body understood before my brain did.

MOVE.

I tried.

My foot slipped.

The world lurched.

BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!

Pain.

Noise.

White.

I didn't know which way was up.

Something hit me.

Then something else.

BANG!

The road flashed beneath me.

Sky.

Road.

Sky.

Rain.

Black.

White.

CRACK!

I couldn't breathe.

I tried.

Nothing.

My ears were screaming.

No—

Not screaming.

Everything was screaming.

SKRRRRRRRRRRRRRRR—

I couldn't feel my legs.

I couldn't feel one of my hands.

I tried to move.

Nothing.

My thoughts were everywhere.

Too fast.

Then too slow.

Mom.

The kitchen.

A cup on the table.

Eat.

Daniel.

You're four minutes late.

No.

Four minutes early.

Whatever.

I almost smiled.

Almost.

Then the ceiling.

That stupid crack.

The mirror.

Average.

Why did I say that?

Why did I always—

The sunlight through the living-room window.

A blanket dragging across the floor.

Six.

I was six.

Wasn't I?

Mom remembered.

I didn't.

I tried to hold onto the image.

A window.

Orange light.

Warm.

Then it was gone.

The classroom came back.

The board.

The phone buzzing.

The comments.

They're right.

No.

They're not.

Someone laughing.

Someone—

The girl.

The notebook.

Her face.

You sure?

My stomach dropped.

I hadn't even—

I'm sorry.

I tried to say it.

Nothing.

My mouth wouldn't—

The road.

The thief.

My phone.

Where was my phone?

That stupid blue case.

I had been chasing—

Why?

Right.

Because it was mine.

My list.

The notes app.

I hadn't finished it.

There was something missing.

Something I'd meant to add.

What was it?

I tried to remember.

My thoughts slipped.

Wake up early.

Already did.

Exercise.

Tomorrow.

Study.

Tomorrow.

Read.

Tomorrow.

Everything was tomorrow.

The word floated there.

Tomorrow.

Tomorrow.

Tomorrow.

Daniel.

School.

Mom.

The assignment.

I still hadn't—

Tomorrow.

My chest tightened.

No.

I had time.

I did.

I was seventeen.

Seventeen wasn't old.

It wasn't.

I hadn't—

I hadn't even started.

The rain fell across my face.

For a second, it didn't feel cold.

Just quiet.

Tap.

A drop landed on my cheek.

Tap.

Another.

The sound seemed very far away.

I thought about Mom again.

Not anything important.

Not some final speech.

Just breakfast.

Her looking at me.

That little almost-smile.

"You're welcome, Jonah Laurence."

I wanted to hear it again.

I wanted—

My thoughts broke apart.

Daniel.

The window.

The list.

The girl.

The phone.

Mom.

The sunrise.

All of them blurred together.

I couldn't tell which memory came first.

I couldn't tell what I was trying to remember.

I only knew I didn't want to forget.

My vision dimmed.

The rain became white lines.

Then grey.

Then nothing I could understand.

I tried to move my hand.

Nothing.

I tried again.

Nothing.

And somewhere inside all that noise, one thought remained.

Not profound.

Not brave.

Not even complete.

Just something I'd believed so easily that I'd never bothered to question it.

Tomorrow.

I waited for the rest of the thought.

It didn't come.

My mind reached for it.

Nothing.

Then, somehow—

...I'll do better.

A tiny thought.

Almost stupid.

Almost hopeful.

I believed it.

Even then.

I believed there would be a tomorrow.

Then—

nothing.
