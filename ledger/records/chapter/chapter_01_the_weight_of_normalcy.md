Chapter One — The Weight of Normalcy
Jonah woke because he heard something.

He didn't know what it was.

For a few seconds, he stayed still with his eyes closed, listening.

The house was quiet.

Not silent. Houses were never completely silent. There was the low hum of the fan, something ticking faintly inside the wall, the occasional sound of a vehicle passing somewhere beyond the street.

Then he heard it again.

A soft scrape.

Jonah opened his eyes.

Nothing.

The room was completely black.

He blinked.

Still nothing.

Usually there was enough light from outside to make out the curtains, the wardrobe, the edge of his desk. Tonight there wasn't even that.

He couldn't see his hand in front of his face.

Power's out.

That was his first thought.

Then he remembered the streetlights outside.

Something about that didn't make sense.

Jonah tried to turn over.

His body didn't move.

He frowned.

He tried again.

Nothing.

For a moment he wondered if he was still asleep.

He could feel the mattress underneath him. The blanket against his chest. The damp warmth at the back of his neck.

He could hear the fan.

He could hear the traffic outside.

He could swallow.

He just couldn't move.

Jonah's heart began to beat harder.

He tried lifting his right hand.

Nothing.

His left.

Nothing.

He pushed against the mattress with his shoulder.

Nothing.

A small breath escaped through his nose.

He tried to speak.

“...Mum?”

The word barely existed.

His throat had produced something, but it sounded so weak that he wasn't sure he'd actually said it.

He swallowed.

Tried again.

“Ma?”

Nothing answered.

Jonah stared into the darkness.

He couldn't see the door.

He couldn't see the ceiling.

He couldn't see anything.

Then the sound came again.

A scrape.

This time it was closer.

Jonah's breathing stopped.

He listened.

Nothing.

His heartbeat filled the room.

He tried moving his fingers.

He concentrated on his right hand.

Move.

Nothing.

Move.

His fingers remained still.

Something made a faint sound near the doorway.

Not a footstep.

More like something brushing against the floor.

Jonah's eyes moved toward it.

He couldn't see the doorway, but he knew where it was.

He had slept in this room for years.

He knew the position of everything.

The door was three or four meters away.

The wardrobe was to his left.

The desk was near the window.

The thing making that sound was somewhere between them.

Jonah stared into the blackness.

A shape appeared.

He wasn't sure it had.

He blinked.

There was nothing.

He kept looking.

Then he saw it again.

Something low to the floor.

His mind immediately tried to make sense of it.

A person.

Someone crouching.

Maybe his mother.

Maybe someone had entered the room.

Jonah tried to move.

Nothing.

The shape shifted.

Jonah's stomach tightened.

It wasn't standing.

It wasn't crouching either.

It seemed to be moving forward without changing height.

Slowly.

He couldn't hear footsteps.

Only the faint drag of something against the floor.

Jonah wanted to close his eyes.

He couldn't.

The shape came closer.

He could see a little more of it now.

Not enough.

Something pale caught the weak light from somewhere outside.

A face.

Maybe.

Jonah stared.

The shape stopped.

He couldn't make out its features.

He couldn't even tell which direction it was facing.

Then it moved its head.

Slowly.

Too slowly.

Jonah's entire body tensed.

He tried to scream.

His throat closed.

Nothing came out.

The thing remained there.

Looking at him.

Or perhaps Jonah only thought it was looking at him.

He couldn't see eyes.

He couldn't see a mouth.

He couldn't see enough to know.

The darkness seemed to swallow the rest.

Jonah's breathing became shallow.

The thing moved again.

Closer.

A hand appeared.

It touched the floor.

The fingers were pale.

Jonah stared at them.

They looked almost normal.

Then one finger bent.

Jonah's stomach dropped.

It bent farther than it should have.

He squeezed his eyes shut.

For a second, there was nothing.

Then something made a soft sound beside the bed.

Jonah opened his eyes.

The shape was gone.

He stared into the darkness.

Nothing.

He couldn't tell whether he was relieved.

Then he felt something near his ankle.

Jonah's whole body jerked.

This time, he moved.

He shot upright so violently that his shoulder struck the wall.

He screamed.

The sound came out raw and loud.

The room was empty.

Jonah scrambled backward until his back hit the headboard.

He stared at the doorway.

Closed.

He looked around.

The wardrobe.

Desk.

Curtains.

Everything was where it should have been.

His breathing came too fast.

He reached for his phone.

His hand knocked it from the bedside table.

It hit the floor.

The screen lit up.

3:17.

Jonah stared at the time.

Then at the door.

Then back at the phone.

His hands were shaking.

He climbed out of bed and switched on the light.

Nothing happened.

He froze.

The power was out.

Jonah stood there in the darkness with his phone lighting one small patch of the room.

He looked toward the floor.

Nothing.

No person.

No pale face.

No hands.

Nothing.

He checked the door.

Locked.

He remembered locking it before going to sleep.

He checked again.

Still locked.

Jonah stayed awake until morning.

At 6:11, his alarm went off.

Jonah reached under his pillow and stopped it.

For a moment he didn't move.

Morning light had returned.

The room looked normal.

That somehow made everything worse.

The crack above his wardrobe was still there. His uniform was hanging over the back of his chair. His schoolbag was on the floor where he'd dropped it.

Nothing had happened.

Nothing that could leave a mark, anyway.

Jonah sat up.

He looked at the door.

Then away.

“Bad dream.”

His voice sounded strange.

He said it again, quieter.

“Just a bad dream.”

He got dressed.

In the bathroom, he brushed his teeth while checking his phone.

Six notifications.

Four were from school groups he'd muted.

One was an advert.

The last was from an unknown number.

You awake?

Jonah stared at it.

He checked the number.

Nothing.

No name.

No profile picture.

He locked his phone.

Then unlocked it again.

The message was still there.

He blocked the number and put the phone away.

His mother was already in the kitchen.

“You're late.”

Jonah looked at the clock.

“It's 6:26.”

“You're supposed to leave before seven.”

“I know.”

She put a plate on the table.

Two slices of bread.

Jonah sat down.

“There's no milk,” she said.

“Okay.”

“You'll have to buy some when you come back.”

“Okay.”

She picked up her phone.

Jonah ate.

The bread stuck to the roof of his mouth.

His mother looked up.

“You hear me?”

“Yes.”

“Then don't forget.”

“I won't.”

She nodded and looked back at her phone.

Jonah finished eating.

He stood.

“I'm going.”

“Hmm.”

He waited.

Nothing else came.

He picked up his bag.

“Bye.”

“Bye.”

She didn't look up.

The walk to school took twenty minutes.

Jonah usually took twenty-three.

He was early.

He didn't know why.

The street was already busy. A bus rolled past with its doors open, the conductor leaning halfway out and shouting for passengers. A woman swept water away from the front of her shop. Someone had left a pile of empty bottles beside a drain.

Normal.

Jonah tried to focus on that.

His phone vibrated.

He pulled it out.

You awake?

The same number.

He stopped walking.

For a second, he simply stared at it.

Then he blocked the number again.

He put the phone away and continued.

At the school gate, two boys from his class were standing beside the security post.

One looked at Jonah.

The other leaned closer.

They said something.

Jonah kept walking.

“Jonah.”

He stopped.

Daniel stood behind him.

“What's up?”

Daniel shrugged.

“Nothing.”

Jonah waited.

Daniel looked somewhere past his shoulder.

“Never mind.”

He walked away.

Jonah watched him go.

Then he entered the school.

The classroom was loud until Jonah walked in.

Not silent.

Just quieter.

A chair scraped.

Someone laughed.

Then stopped.

Jonah went to his desk.

The boy beside him moved his bag from the other chair.

Jonah sat down.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

The teacher came in.

Nobody said anything about the messages.

Nobody needed to.

Jonah could feel phones appearing whenever the teacher turned toward the board.

His own phone buzzed inside his pocket.

He ignored it.

It buzzed again.

He checked beneath the desk.

A screenshot.

The same accusation from the night before.

Someone had reposted it.

The wording had changed.

The story had changed with it.

Jonah read it once.

Then locked his phone.

“Jonah.”

He looked up.

“Yes, sir?”

“Are you following?”

“Yes.”

The teacher watched him for another second.

“Good.”

He turned back toward the board.

Jonah looked down.

His pen hadn't moved.

He forced himself to start writing.

By lunch, he was hungry.

He sat beneath the covered walkway behind one of the buildings.

It was quieter there.

He opened his container.

Rice and stew.

He ate.

A group of students passed.

One looked at him.

Another whispered.

The first laughed.

Jonah kept eating.

His phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

He opened it.

You should watch your back.

Jonah stared at the message.

He looked around.

The walkway was almost empty.

A teacher stood at the far end talking to two students. A security guard was smoking beside the gate.

Nobody was looking at him.

Jonah typed:

Who is this?

He stared at the words.

Then deleted them.

He put the phone away.

His appetite was gone.

He closed the container.

Rain started before the last period ended.

By the time school finished, water was already running along the road.

Jonah waited beneath the awning.

Students crowded around the entrance.

Someone complained about the rain.

Someone else called their father.

A girl beside Jonah laughed at something on her phone.

His phone vibrated.

His mother.

Where are you?

School.

The reply came immediately.

Come home.

Jonah looked at the rain.

Okay.

When it eased, he left.

He got home at 6:03.

His mother was in the living room.

“You're late.”

“There was rain.”

“You couldn't call?”

“My phone was on silent.”

She looked at him.

“You always have an excuse.”

Jonah said nothing.

He took his bag upstairs.

The television volume increased behind him.

He closed his bedroom door.

Dropped his bag.

Sat on the bed.

His phone was still in his hand.

He checked the unknown number.

Blocked.

The school group.

Muted.

The post.

Still there.

He stared at it for a while.

Then closed it.

He opened a video.

Watched half of it.

Closed it.

Opened another.

Closed that too.

At 8:14, his mother called him for dinner.

He went downstairs.

“How was school?”

“Fine.”

“How was your test?”

“Fine.”

“Did you study?”

“A little.”

She nodded.

They ate.

Neither of them spoke again.

At 12:41, Jonah was still awake.

He'd been trying to sleep for nearly an hour.

He turned onto his side.

Pulled the blanket higher.

Checked the door.

Locked.

He checked his phone.

12:42.

He put it face down beside the bed.

The house settled around him.

A pipe clicked.

Something moved downstairs.

A car passed outside.

Then nothing.

Jonah opened his eyes.

He stared at the ceiling.

The crack above the wardrobe was barely visible.

He remembered the room from the night before.

The darkness.

The shape.

The hand.

He told himself not to think about it.

He closed his eyes.

The house was quiet.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

His breathing slowed.

His thoughts became less clear.

And then he heard the scrape again.

Jonah opened his eyes.

He couldn't see anything.

He tried to move.

Nothing happened.

His heart slammed against his ribs.

He tried again.

Nothing.

The room was completely black.

No streetlight.

No moonlight.

No phone.

Nothing.

He could hear his own breathing.

Then something breathed from somewhere in the room.

Jonah stopped.

He listened.

There it was again.

Not beside him.

Not at the door.

Somewhere in the darkness.

A slow, wet breath.

Jonah tried to turn his head.

He couldn't.

Something shifted across the floor.

He couldn't see it.

He couldn't tell where it was.

The sound stopped.

Silence.

Jonah stared into the blackness.

Then, very close to his bed, something whispered his name.

“Jonah.”

His eyes widened.

And this time, he knew the voice wasn't his mother's.
