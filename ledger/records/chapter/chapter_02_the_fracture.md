Chapter Two — The Fracture
I woke up before my alarm.

Not by much.

My phone said 6:17.

My alarm was set for 6:30.

I stared at the ceiling for a few seconds, trying to remember why I was awake.

Then I remembered.

I looked toward my bedroom door.

It was closed.

I didn't move.

Nothing happened.

After a while, I let out the breath I'd apparently been holding.

“Okay.”

My voice sounded stupidly quiet.

I reached for my phone.

No messages.

No notifications.

Nothing.

I checked anyway.

Then I checked again.

I didn't know what I expected to find.

Maybe a message from whoever had been sending those things last night.

Maybe some explanation.

Maybe proof that I hadn't imagined the whole thing.

There was nothing.

I put the phone against my chest and stared at the ceiling again.

There was a thin crack running across it, just above where I slept. I'd noticed it months ago. It wasn't getting bigger or anything. It was just there.

I'd spent enough mornings looking at it that I could almost trace the whole thing from memory.

My hair was probably a mess.

Actually, definitely a mess.

I ran a hand through it and felt a few strands sticking out in completely different directions.

Great.

I got out of bed.

My room looked exactly how I'd left it.

School uniform hanging off the back of my chair.

Bag on the floor.

Two textbooks underneath my desk.

One sock beside the bed.

The other sock had disappeared sometime during the week.

I had accepted that I would never see it again.

I picked up my uniform and got dressed.

In the mirror, I looked tired.

Not dramatically tired.

Just seventeen.

Messy hair. Slightly puffy eyes. Nothing particularly memorable about my face unless someone was already looking at me.

I leaned closer to the mirror.

“Ugly.”

I paused.

“Not ugly.”

Another pause.

“Average.”

That sounded fair.

I fixed my hair anyway.

My mother was already in the kitchen.

She was drinking tea while looking at her phone.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

“You're going to school?”

I looked at her.

“Yes.”

“I mean, are you ready?”

“Obviously.”

She nodded and went back to her phone.

That was pretty much breakfast.

I opened the cupboard, found bread, and made myself something to eat.

The kitchen was small enough that two people could make it feel crowded without actually getting in each other's way.

The refrigerator made a low humming noise.

A tap somewhere near the sink dripped every few seconds.

Outside, somebody was already arguing with somebody else.

Normal morning.

I liked mornings like this.

Because nobody was paying attention to me.

I finished eating, grabbed my bag, and left.

The air outside was already warm.

Our street was busy in the way it always was in the morning. People heading somewhere. Cars squeezing past each other. Shops opening. Someone sweeping dirt from directly in front of a shop and somehow putting half of it back onto the road.

I walked with my hands in my pockets.

For the first few minutes, I didn't think about anything.

Then my phone buzzed.

I stopped.

It was Daniel.

Daniel: where are you

I replied.

Me: outside

Three seconds later:

Daniel: revolutionary answer

I smiled.

Me: shut up

Daniel: hurry up idiot

I put my phone away.

Daniel was waiting near the corner when I got there.

He was around my height, dark-skinned, with short hair and his school shirt already looking like he'd been wearing it for three hours instead of twenty minutes.

He looked at me.

Then at my hair.

Then back at me.

“What?”

“You look like you fought a pillow.”

“I won.”

“Clearly.”

I pushed him.

He laughed.

We started walking.

Daniel could talk about absolutely nothing for an impressive amount of time.

This morning it was football.

Then a teacher.

Then some video he'd seen.

Then football again.

I mostly listened.

Occasionally I disagreed just to annoy him.

“You're wrong.”

“I haven't even finished.”

“Still wrong.”

“You don't even know what I'm saying.”

“I know enough.”

“That doesn't even make sense.”

“It doesn't have to.”

He shook his head.

“You're actually annoying.”

“Thank you.”

School was school.

The gate was crowded.

Students were everywhere, uniforms mixing together until everybody looked like part of the same moving mass.

I hated that part.

Not because I hated people.

I just hated having to squeeze through them while pretending I wasn't trying to squeeze through them.

Inside, the classroom was already getting loud.

Chairs scraped against the floor.

Someone was laughing at the back.

A fan turned slowly above us, making a clicking sound every few rotations.

I sat down.

Daniel dropped into the seat beside me.

“Did you do the assignment?”

I looked at him.

“No.”

He looked relieved.

“Good.”

“You didn't either?”

“No.”

“Why are you relieved?”

“I thought you were going to make me feel bad.”

“That would've required me doing it.”

“Exactly.”

We laughed.

For a few minutes, everything was normal.

Actually normal.

No strange noises.

No anonymous messages.

No staring at the ceiling at three in the morning wondering whether I was awake.

Just school.

And honestly?

I preferred it that way.

Maya came over before the teacher arrived.

She had her hair tied back neatly and carried two notebooks against her chest.

“You people didn't do the assignment?”

Daniel immediately pointed at me.

“He didn't.”

“Traitor.”

“You're the one who said you didn't do it.”

Maya looked at both of us.

“You're both idiots.”

“That's fair,” I said.

She smiled and went back to her seat.

That was it.

Nothing dramatic.

Sometimes people were just people.

I think I'd forgotten that lately.

By break time, I'd almost stopped thinking about the night before.

Almost.

Daniel had convinced us to go behind the science block because apparently that was where people went when they wanted to waste ten minutes.

There wasn't anything particularly interesting there.

Just a concrete wall, some grass, a few old pipes and enough shade to make it cooler than the main courtyard.

We sat around talking.

Someone started telling a story about a teacher.

Another person interrupted halfway through.

Then everybody started arguing about what had actually happened.

I laughed so hard at one point that I nearly choked.

Daniel slapped my back.

“You good?”

I waved him off, still coughing.

“Yeah. Your stupid story just tried to kill me.”

“That's not my fault.”

“You're literally the one who told it.”

“Yeah, but you didn't have to laugh that hard.”

I gave him a look.

He shrugged.

“That's on you.”

I laughed again.

For those few minutes, I forgot about my phone.

That was probably the best part.

Then I checked it.

I had three notifications.

One was from a group chat.

The other two were screenshots.

I opened the first.

Someone had posted something about me.

Nothing technically new.

Someone had taken something I'd said, changed the wording, and made it sound worse.

The comments underneath were worse.

Some people believed it.

Some people didn't.

A few people were clearly just enjoying themselves.

I stared at the screen.

Daniel noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

He leaned over.

I turned the phone away.

“Seriously?”

“Yeah.”

He didn't push.

That was one thing I liked about him.

He could be annoying as hell, but sometimes he knew when to leave something alone.

I locked my phone.

For the rest of lunch, I talked.

I laughed.

I even forgot about it again.

Then somebody across the courtyard looked at me.

I looked away first.

I hated that I did that.

The strange thing about rumors was that you never knew exactly how far they'd travelled.

You'd hear one version.

Then another.

Then somebody would mention something completely different.

Sometimes I'd wonder what people actually believed.

Sometimes I'd wonder what they were saying when I wasn't there.

That was the part I hated most.

Not knowing.

If somebody hated me to my face, at least I knew where I stood.

When I got home that afternoon, I checked my phone again.

Nothing new.

I threw it onto my bed.

Five minutes later, I picked it back up.

Still nothing.

I put it down again.

Then picked it up.

“Why am I doing this?”

My phone remained silent.

I opened a game instead.

That worked for about twenty minutes.

Then I got bored.

I wandered into the kitchen.

My mother was there.

“How was school?”

“Fine.”

“Anything happen?”

I almost said yes.

Instead I opened the fridge.

“No.”

She nodded.

That was the end of the conversation.

I grabbed a drink and went back to my room.

I sat at my desk and stared at my homework.

I did maybe three questions.

Then I got distracted.

Then I remembered I had a test coming up.

Then I decided I would study later.

I knew perfectly well what “later” meant.

It meant tonight.

Then tomorrow.

Then suddenly it was the night before the test and I was wondering why I'd been given an education in the first place.

I opened my notebook again.

My phone buzzed.

I froze.

I looked at the screen.

Unknown number.

For a second I didn't open it.

Then I did.

You looked happy today.

I stared at it.

Another message arrived.

Enjoy it while you can.

My stomach tightened.

I blocked the number.

Then I put my phone face down.

I told myself it was probably someone messing with me.

It was probably nothing.

I hated that phrase.

Probably nothing.

It could mean anything.

I went back to my homework.

For the next ten minutes, I didn't read a single word.

That night, I ate dinner.

I watched something on my phone.

I argued with Daniel over text about something completely stupid.

He sent me a terrible meme.

I told him it was terrible.

He said I had no taste.

I sent one back.

He stopped replying.

Victory.

It felt good.

Small things like that mattered more than they probably should have.

Maybe that was the problem.

I had a lot of small things.

Small wins.

Small embarrassments.

Small reasons to get angry.

Small reasons to laugh.

And sometimes one bad thing could make all of them feel insignificant.

But not always.

Sometimes I could forget.

Sometimes I could just be me.

I liked those moments.

I wanted more of them.

I just didn't really know how to make them happen.

I finished my homework eventually.

Mostly.

I put everything away and got ready for bed.

Before sleeping, I checked my phone one last time.

Nothing.

I stared at the screen.

Then I turned it off.

For a moment, I thought about last night.

The room.

The darkness.

The feeling that something had been there.

My chest tightened.

I told myself it had been a nightmare.

That made sense.

Nightmares made more sense than anything else.

I wasn't going to sit there inventing monsters.

I wasn't that stupid.

I got into bed.

The room was quiet.

I pulled the blanket up.

Tomorrow was Thursday.

I had school.

Daniel would probably annoy me.

Maya would probably complain about something.

I'd probably put off studying again.

Nothing had changed.

That thought should have made me feel better.

It did.

For a little while.

I closed my eyes.

And slept.
