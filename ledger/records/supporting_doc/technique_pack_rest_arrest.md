# Rest & Arrest — Stillness, Suspension & Timing of Attention

Rest is a controlled reduction of narrative motion. Arrest is the moment movement or interpretation seems to stop under pressure. Neither means “nothing happens”: a still passage can change attention, allow emotion to register, or make an unresolved question harder to avoid. This is a project-specific craft label, not a universal taxonomy.

## What can be arrested?

- Physical action: freeze, listen, wait, refuse to move.
- Information: delay an answer while evidence accumulates.
- Interpretation: observe a detail without understanding it yet.
- Emotional response: delay naming what happened.
- Narrative time: sustain attention on a small interval.
- Social exchange: let a pause expose discomfort or a mismatch.

## Ledger cases

- **Chapter 1:** Jonah repeatedly listens and tests whether he can move. Each pause preserves uncertainty because his senses do not settle the question.
- **Chapter 4:** time loses its ordinary markers; later, grass, wind, crickets, and headlights restore local sensory time.
- **Chapter 7:** clocks, proctor commands, pencil movement, erasing, and collection hold the examination in a controlled procedural rhythm.
- **Chapter 8:** the heating kettle and still apartment create space after the exam, but the quiet exposes the dossier's biographical gaps.

## Procedure

Name the pressure that makes the moment worth holding. Choose an anchor: sound, object, bodily sensation, clock, repeated action, or unresolved question. Decide what changes during the pause: knowledge, attention, expectation, emotional recognition, or tolerance for uncertainty. Control duration through sentence span, paragraph breaks, repetition, and new information. Release through action, answer, interruption, reframing, or deliberate non-resolution. Compress the passage if nothing changes.

A short sentence can arrest movement; a long sentence can sustain a slow sweep. Tiny paragraphs can accelerate reading rather than slow it. Effect depends on syntax and context, not one formatting trick.

## Failure modes

- A dramatic pause after every sentence.
- Repeatedly saying a character is still without changing the meaning of stillness.
- Withholding all information and calling the result suspense.
- Atmospheric delay without narrative pressure.
- Explaining every emotional beat, leaving no room for the reader.

## Test

Read aloud. Mark natural pauses and changes in the scene's question. Remove one repeated beat. If pressure remains unchanged, cut it; if the moment loses the time needed for a clue to register, preserve or reshape it.
