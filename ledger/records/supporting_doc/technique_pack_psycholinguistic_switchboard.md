# Psycholinguistic Switchboard — Reader Prediction, Inference & Coherence

Readers do not receive a story as a complete database. They build a working understanding by connecting sentences, retrieving earlier details, making inferences, predicting likely developments, and revising interpretations when new evidence arrives. This pack offers craft questions; it does not claim to control every reader's brain.

## Mechanisms to inspect

- **Prediction:** what continuation does the passage make plausible?
- **Prediction error:** what new detail requires revision?
- **Bridging inference:** what unstated link is needed for coherence?
- **Causal inference:** does the text support the relation readers may infer?
- **Retrieval:** does a recurring image reactivate earlier information?
- **Ambiguity:** is uncertainty intentional, or is basic action unclear?
- **Cognitive load:** how many new entities, relations, time shifts, or sensory changes arrive together?
- **Schema revision:** what prior assumption is challenged?

## Ledger cases

- **Chapter 1:** expected bedroom safety is undermined by restricted perception and an unresolved shape.
- **Chapters 2–3:** Jonah interprets laughter as being about him, then recognizes he lacks evidence.
- **Chapter 4:** familiar astronomy provides a starting schema; impossible geometry makes it insufficient; ordinary bodily sensation restores anchors.
- **Chapter 7:** repeated exam procedures reduce uncertainty about what happens next, making small sounds salient.
- **Chapter 8:** one social prediction works, another fails because Ira prepares for a question that was not asked.

## Procedure

Record what the reader knows at this point. Identify the likely expectation. Decide whether to fulfill, delay, complicate, or violate it. Ensure enough cues exist for necessary inferences. Separate productive uncertainty from accidental confusion. Track how many new facts arrive together. Revisit earlier cues after a reveal.

Evaluate with questions and comparative reading: what did a reader expect, infer, recall, or find confusing? Do not fabricate certainty or reduce all response to a score.

## Failure modes

Treating all readers as identical; equating surprise with quality; hiding information needed to understand action; confusing character inference with required reader inference; invoking neuroscience as decoration; claiming fragments simulate a precise neurological state.

## Research grounding

Wong, Reichle & Veldre, [review of prediction in reading](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02588-z); Trabasso & Suh, [explanatory coherence and inference](https://doi.org/10.1080/01638539309544827); Horváth, [causal expectations, suspense, and surprise](https://doi.org/10.14232/ncognito/2023.2.35-46). Treat these as foundations for testable questions, not universal recipes.
