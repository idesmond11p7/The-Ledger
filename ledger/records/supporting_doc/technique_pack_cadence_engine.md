# Cadence Engine — Prose Rhythm, Sentence Motion & Sonic Texture

Cadence is the felt movement of language over time. It emerges from syntax, sentence length, clause arrangement, stress, punctuation, repetition, phonetic texture, paragraphing, and expectations. “Short sentences for action, long sentences for description” is a crude heuristic, not a law.

## Controls

- **Span:** short, medium, long, variable.
- **Syntax:** coordination, subordination, interruption, accumulation, delayed main clause.
- **Stress:** where emphasis falls when read.
- **Sound:** alliteration, assonance, consonance, onomatopoeia.
- **Repetition:** recurring words, structures, or beats.
- **Punctuation and grouping:** commas, periods, dashes, paragraph breaks.
- **Expectation:** sustain, delay, complete, or break an established pattern.

## Ledger cases

- **Chapter 1:** repeated failed movement beats and “Nothing” create a testing rhythm that repeatedly fails to provide relief.
- **Chapter 3:** onomatopoeia, pulse-like beats, isolated sensory words, and fragments narrow the chase and collision sequence as attention breaks apart.
- **Chapter 4:** long descriptive sentences accumulate spatial relations during cosmic scale passages; the same density would be less useful for immediate action.
- **Chapter 6:** station and train sounds make movement tactile; the isolated “GONG” changes the cadence before the exam.
- **Chapter 7:** repeated paper distribution and graphite sounds create controlled procedural rhythm.

## Procedure

Identify the scene's physical and emotional movement. Mark natural stresses aloud. Find the existing sentence pattern. Decide whether to sustain, accelerate, decelerate, interrupt, or reverse it. Let sound serve action, voice, mood, or structure. Vary the pattern when attention changes. Recheck meaning so that rhythm does not destroy action logic.

Prose can have cadence without becoming lineated poetry. Poetry may use stress, pause, repetition, and page form as part of its organization.

## Failure modes

- Every sentence is fragmented, so no fragment feels exceptional.
- Onomatopoeia substitutes for clear verbs.
- Sentence length is manipulated mechanically.
- Alliteration calls attention to itself without adding meaning.
- Every sentence gets its own paragraph.
- Ornate sound obscures the speaker's meaning.

## Tests

Read aloud. Track sentence-length sequences across a paragraph. Remove a sound effect and check whether action remains legible. Compare a long-syntax version with a short-syntax version instead of assuming one is superior.

## Research grounding

Alan Roberts, [“Rhythm in Prose and the Serial Correlation of Sentence Lengths”](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article-abstract/11/1/33/969582), examines sentence-length patterns across prose. [“Reading Poetry and Prose: Eye Movements and Acoustic Evidence”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2021.2015188) reports differences in studied oral readings. Neither establishes one ideal cadence for all writing.
