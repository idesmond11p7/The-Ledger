# Chapter 9 — Spatial Simulation & Errand Decision Record

**Status:** Proposed design v1; geography is chapter-specific until confirmed by a canonical Kuoh map.  
**Related:** `chapter_09_scene_architecture_v1.md`

## 1. Locked design decision

The chapter's errand is a **message delivery** for the café acquaintance introduced in Chapter 8. The acquaintance is helping with a family obligation and cannot leave. He asks Ira to take a short written message to an adult contact at a small repair / supply shop in the Minami-Kuoh side of town. The message is ordinary and the errand has no arbitrary deadline. The shop is open during normal business hours; the reason to go now is convenience and the acquaintance's practical need, not a contrived countdown.

This task is preferable to a mysterious parcel or a valuable object:
- a folded note is easy to carry and creates no implausible trust problem;
- the request can be small enough for a new acquaintance;
- the recipient can confirm completion in a natural way;
- the destination can plausibly sit near mixed-use streets and the edge of a wooded area;
- the task gives Ira a reason to use a map and navigate independently.

The acquaintance should not be called a close friend at the start. The relationship becomes a friendship through the shared task and subsequent contact. The Chapter 8 continuity bridge must provide the acquaintance's name and a legitimate reason for Ira to recognize him. Until that patch exists, use a role label in planning documents and leave the name blank in prose.

The errand should be presented as a request, not a mission briefing. No HUD, quest marker, countdown, reward screen, or exaggerated urgency.

## 3. Movement and camera simulation

### Leg A — apartment to information point

**Initial framing:** close, embodied view. Ira checks the folded map, the copied address, his wallet, and his dead phone. The objects matter because he is deciding what information he can rely on without a functioning phone.

**Movement:** leave the building, descend the stairs, turn onto the residential lane. Let the familiar Chapter 8 street become a route rather than repeating its entire description.

**Spatial cues:** building fronts, bicycles, overhead wires, street corner, commercial signs. Ira should use a landmark to confirm direction. Do not narrate a complete overhead map.

**Psychological cue:** he is pleased that he has prepared, but the pleasure is cautious. The map is not certainty; it is a tool.

### Leg B — directory / information point

**Purpose:** fulfill Chapter 8's stated intention before the errand takes over. Ira checks a printed directory or asks a municipal information worker where the named annex is generally located. The information point does not magically validate the organization. It can confirm a listing or provide a route, while the legitimacy of the dossier remains unresolved.

**Camera:** establish the counter, queue, directory, Ira's position, and the worker's line of sight. A brief social misreading can occur, but do not repeat the Chapter 8 café exchange.

**Transition:** Ira compares the address with the map and chooses a route that uses a bus. The route should be a conscious choice, not an arbitrary cut.

### Leg C — bus corridor

**Boarding:** show the stop, route number or destination display only if the Writer has grounded it in the map. Ira checks the sign before boarding. This echoes Chapter 6's Local/Rapid correction without repeating the exact train mistake.

**Interior:** the bus is a moving spatial frame. The window overlays reflections of passengers and interior fittings on the city outside. Nearby poles and storefronts slide quickly; distant buildings move more slowly. Turns change which landmarks remain visible.

**Attention:** Ira watches for the stop and occasionally loses the landmark behind a vehicle or building. He checks again rather than assuming.

**Sound:** engine vibration enters through the floor and seat structure; stop announcements are shaped by the speaker and bus interior; doors hiss and the street sound expands when they open. Do not make every sound a separate paragraph.

### Leg D — mixed-use district

**Wide-to-close progression:** first show the district as a change in density and signage; then let Ira notice individual storefronts, adult groups, pedestrians, and traffic.

**Adult nightlife:** the atmosphere is carried by confident adult social behavior, close conversation, public affection, late-opening storefronts, and perhaps visibly tipsy adults. Ira notices the social temperature without eroticizing people. The scene is about his unfamiliarity and Kuoh's social character.

**Hospital node:** use the hospital as a spatial landmark and a practical contrast to the nightlife strip. Ambulance access, lit entrance, people arriving or leaving, and the boundary between public pavement and controlled grounds can give it a distinct identity. Do not insert a medical emergency merely to make it dramatic.

**Route decision:** Ira must choose a turn using the map and visible landmarks. This is the main proof that he has improved since Chapter 6.

### Leg E — errand destination and cutscene

**Exterior:** the recipient's shop should be small, functional, and integrated into the street. The sign, shutter, delivery access, window display, and door establish what kind of place it is.

**Task:** Ira delivers the folded message. The recipient reads it, responds naturally, and confirms the errand is complete. The message itself should be mundane; its contents need not become a new plot mystery.

**Cutscene:** one bounded heteroperspective shot may reveal the recipient's reaction or a brief exchange with someone in the shop that Ira does not fully hear. It should deepen the human scene, not explain the supernatural plot.

**Exit:** Ira leaves with the practical satisfaction of completing a task. The city is still ordinary. The forest is visible from the destination or from a nearby public path.

## 4. Forest-edge geometry

The wooded area must be visible and plausible from the errand destination. It should not be a deep wilderness suddenly adjoining the busiest street. Use a transitional edge:
- a low retaining wall or drainage channel;
- a public path or service road;
- a change in pavement quality;
- trees that partly screen the slope;
- a sign or barrier whose practical meaning Ira can understand;
- a gradual loss of traffic visibility as the path bends.

The four-year-old child and responsible adult are near the public edge. The cat bolts through an opening or along a path that the adult cannot safely follow while managing the child. Ira is close enough to see the route and follow.

**Do not have the adult abandon the child or casually ask a stranger to rescue the cat.** The adult calls after the cat, tries to keep the child from following, and may call to Ira because he is the nearest older person already moving in the same direction. Ira decides to continue because he can still see the cat and the path appears short.

## 5. Boundary-crossing simulation

The boundary is not a visible gate in the ordinary sense. It is a threshold event that Ira fails to classify.

### Before crossing

- City traffic remains faintly audible.
- The path is still identifiable as a public route.
- Ira can see the cat intermittently.
- Trees, ground, and light have ordinary spatial continuity.

### During the crossing

- A bend or stand of trees blocks the last stable view of the street.
- Ira follows the cat around it.
- The expected return view of the path is not where he expects it.
- One familiar sound persists but no longer has a reliable direction.
- The apparent distance to the cat or next tree fails to match his pace.
- The forest becomes dimmer in a way that changes depth and contrast, not merely exposure.

Do not write a sentence such as “I crossed into the astral realm.” That is author knowledge. Ira only experiences a series of mismatches.

### After crossing

- The cat remains his immediate goal.
- The forest's geometry is less stable.
- The city has disappeared from view and sound.
- Ira's map is no longer useful because it describes the ordinary route, not the overlay.
- He notices the desolation only after the mismatch becomes too large to ignore.

## 6. Stalking sequence as a 3D attention problem

The entities should occupy the edges of Ira's perceptual field rather than stand in front of him.

| Beat | Ira's attention | Reader's evidence | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cat movement ahead | A second movement interrupts the background between trunks | Whether it was a body, branch, or perceptual error |
| 2 | Sound to one side | The sound seems too close for the visible distance | Source and direction |
| 3 | Head turn | The place he checked is empty | Whether something moved or was never there |
| 4 | Return to cat | A dark vertical form is briefly occluded by a trunk | Identity and distance |
| 5 | Search for cat | A sound occurs behind him without the expected echo | Acoustic cause |
| 6 | House appears | A shape is visible beyond the trees, but its distance is hard to judge | Whether the house was always there |
| 7 | Entry | A pressure of attention seems to follow him indoors | Whether any entity crossed the threshold |

The reader may infer pursuit before Ira does, but the prose must supply evidence. Do not state that the entities are stalking him in first-person narration.

## 7. House and upstairs geometry

The house needs a coherent floor plan:
- front or side entrance;
- narrow entry space;
- ground-floor room or corridor;
- stairwell;
- upper landing;
- room with damaged floorboards;
- visible gap or weakened section.

Ira enters for one clear reason tied to the cat's trace or the need to search. The stairs should be physically navigable. The upper room should not appear as an unrelated new set.

### Void rendering

The purple phenomenon should be seen through a constrained opening, gap, or damaged portion of the upper structure. Its light interacts with the room:
- a purple edge travels over dust and broken wood;
- nearby surfaces receive color unevenly;
- the floor's holes reveal depth without showing a stable bottom;
- the chant-like sound seems to occupy more space than the room can contain;
- thunder or a low impact changes the room's acoustic response.

Do not define whether the phenomenon is a portal, a storm, a realm, a ritual, or an entity. Ira does not know.

### Fall mechanics

The weakened floorboard flexes under Ira's weight. A board cracks, another loses support, and the opening widens. Ira's body shifts; he reaches for a support that also gives way or catches only briefly. He falls through the weakened section, hits a lower surface, and scrambles toward the exit.

The tooth necklace catches on a seam, pocket edge, belt loop, or loose fabric during the fall. The exact attachment must be physically plausible and visible to the reader, but not noticed by Ira.
