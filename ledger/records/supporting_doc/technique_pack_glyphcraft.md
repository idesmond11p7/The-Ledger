# Glyphcraft — Typography, Punctuation & the Shape of Reading

Typography becomes narrative when visible form changes emphasis, timing, voice, interruption, or the reader's relationship to the material. In ordinary prose, typography often works best when it recedes. If every line is loud, no emphasis remains.

## Controls

Capitalization; punctuation; paragraph breaks; isolated words or lines; spacing and indentation; formatting for in-world documents, messages, and notes; and lineation when the text is genuinely using verse or visual composition.

A deviation is meaningful only against a recognizable baseline.

## Ledger cases

- **Chapter 3:** isolated sounds and uppercase impacts make the collision visually abrupt. If every movement is capitalized, the impact sequence becomes uniform.
- **Chapter 7:** standalone sounds such as “Slap.” and the bell strike interrupt the procedural texture.
- **Chapters 7–8:** repeating the previous chapter's last sentence at the next chapter's opening creates a structural hinge through placement, not unusual type.

## Procedure

Identify the reading effect. Choose the least intrusive form that achieves it. Establish the baseline, apply the change, and check whether it alters interpretation rather than decorating the sentence. Test the text in its intended display environment, because spacing may render differently. Preserve legibility.

Typography can suggest intensity, hesitation, interruption, or procedural language, but cannot encode an exact vocal performance by itself. Context and syntax still matter.

## Failure modes

- Excessive uppercase makes every beat compete.
- Ellipses replace actual hesitation or omission.
- Every sentence becomes its own paragraph.
- A visual trick depends on spacing that collapses elsewhere.
- Formatting simulates emotion that the scene has not established.

## Test

Remove unusual formatting while keeping the words. Restore only the strongest choices. If the stripped version is clearer and more forceful, the original likely overused emphasis.

## Research grounding

Scott and Jackson, [“Typography, expectations and procedures”](https://benjamins.com/catalog/ftl.8.06sco), discuss typographic choices as interpretive cues relative to expectations. Daniel Matore, [The Graphics of Verse](https://academic.oup.com/book/55287), examines spacing, indentation, alignment, and typeface in experimental poetry.
