## 1. Non-negotiable cause

Ira accepts the Chapter 9 errand because of the specific psychological state and resolve established in Chapter 8. He is trying to get himself together, confront his procrastination, and stop allowing fear or anticipated failure to decide what he attempts.

The original Chapter 9 author brief says Ira is given an errand that he ties to the emotional session/ego he had in Chapter 8, and that this is why he is motivated to take it. This is the causal reason for the decision, not optional subtext.

Chapter 8 makes the pattern concrete: Ira recognises that deciding to improve gives him a temporary burst of energy and an appealing imagined future, while the difficult part is following through once that feeling fades and an inconvenience appears. He recognises that he has been failing in advance to protect his pride. His resolve is not to become fearless; it is to stop letting fear decide what he is allowed to attempt.

The errand is the first immediate behavioural test of that resolve. The acquaintance creates the opportunity. The Chapter 8 resolve supplies the motive.

## 2. Required causal sequence

**Chapter 8 self-recognition of the decision-without-follow-through loop → imperfect resolve to act despite fear and uncertainty → friendly Chapter 8 acquaintance offers a real errand → Ira recognises this as an immediate chance to follow through → he accepts despite hesitation → the errand carries him through Kuoh → the adventure and horror sequence unfolds.**

Do not reverse or dilute this chain.

## 3. Acquaintance selection

**Selected candidate for Chapter 9 planning: the first/inquiring teenage boy in the Chapter 8 café scene.**

Source-supported behaviour in the recovered Chapter 8 text:
- He notices Ira and makes an incorrect assumption based on Ira's hair.
- He apologises directly and without making the mistake into a major social event.
- He casually asks whether Ira lives nearby.
- He is trying to find a meeting place with his companion and discusses a hand-drawn map.
- When Ira answers awkwardly, the boy accepts the answer and moves on rather than humiliating or interrogating him.

This is a brief, friendly first acquaintance, not an established close friendship. It is the strongest available candidate because he actually speaks to Ira and demonstrates low-friction social behaviour. The second boy is the companion who corrects him; he is not the primary candidate unless a later source gives him a stronger interaction.

**Character-development direction (proposal, not established canon):** build on the first boy's existing ease and lack of social defensiveness. He may be energetic, enthusiastic, and visibly hyped when discussing the errand or something he wants to accomplish. His energy should feel like an extension of his casual, unselfconscious manner, not a personality replacement. He can be friendly and forward without treating Ira as an old friend. The user delegated the detailed character concept to the Circulator; do not return the task to the user merely because the source does not supply a name or full biography.

A name, precise age, appearance details beyond what the source establishes, family situation, and exact errand remain proposed details until developed and checked. Do not present invented details as Chapter 8 facts.

## 4. Psychological execution

- Ira has not overcome procrastination permanently.
- His Chapter 8 resolve is vulnerable to the same failure pattern it is trying to correct: an energising intention that could evaporate when the first inconvenience appears.
- “Think ahead. Pay attention. Stop walking into things unprepared” is an imperfect self-directive, not a cure and not a guarantee of correct predictions.
- Ira has not become fearless, socially fluent, confident, or suddenly heroic.
- He may hesitate, overthink the request, question why he agreed, and feel the scale of the errand growing after he has started.
- The meaningful change is behavioural: he follows through on an available opportunity instead of postponing action to protect his pride.
- Keep the decision small in outward presentation but significant to Ira.
- Show the connection through the thought/action relationship. Do not add a clumsy explanatory speech that repeats Chapter 8.

## 5. Errand design

- The errand must be modest and credible for a new acquaintance to ask.
- The request must be made by the selected Chapter 8 acquaintance, not by an invented school friend or unrelated courier.
- The acquaintance's friendliness makes the request socially plausible; it is not sufficient motivation for Ira's acceptance.
- The errand should give Ira a concrete opportunity to enact his Chapter 8 resolve and create a natural route through Kuoh.
- Do not use Kenji, school lockers, replacement clamps, or a framing-shop delivery as the bridge unless the actual authoritative Chapter 8 source establishes those details.
- Exact errand, destination, and route remain open for deliberate design. Do not let an exploratory proposal silently become canon.
