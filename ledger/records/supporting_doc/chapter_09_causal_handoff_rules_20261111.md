## 1. The non-negotiable cause

Ira accepts the Chapter 9 mission because of the specific psychological state and resolve established in Chapter 8. He is trying to get himself together, confront his procrastination, and stop allowing fear or anticipated failure to decide what he attempts.

The original Chapter 9 author brief states that Ira is given an errand which he ties to the emotional session/ego he had in Chapter 8, and that this is why he is motivated to take it. This is not optional subtext. It is the causal reason for the decision.

The acquaintance is the trigger/opportunity. The Chapter 8 resolve is the motive.

## 2. Causal sequence

**Chapter 8 self-recognition → limited resolve to act → friendly acquaintance offers a real errand → Ira recognizes an immediate chance to act instead of retreating/postponing → he accepts despite uncertainty → the errand carries him through Kuoh → the adventure/horror sequence unfolds.**

Do not reverse this chain. Do not make the acquaintance's friendliness, generic helpfulness, desire for inclusion, altruism, or curiosity the primary cause.

## 3. Psychological execution

- Ira has not overcome procrastination permanently.
- He has not become fearless, socially fluent, confident, or suddenly heroic.
- He can hesitate and mentally negotiate with himself.
- The meaningful change is behavioural: he chooses to proceed rather than protect himself through avoidance.
- The decision should feel like a small but personally significant act, not a grand vow or speech.
- The reader should understand the connection through the scene's thought/action relationship. Avoid a clumsy explanatory paragraph that repeats Chapter 8.

## 4. Acquaintance and errand

- Select a person who actually appears and interacts with Ira in the authoritative Chapter 8.
- The person must have behaved in a friendly manner; the relationship is a new acquaintance, not an established close friendship unless the source says otherwise.
- Do not invent a name, contact method, promise, or prior intimacy.
- The errand should be modest and credible for this acquaintance to ask.
- Its function is to give Ira an immediate, concrete opportunity to enact his Chapter 8 resolve and to create a natural route through Kuoh.
- Exact identity and errand details remain open until matched against the authoritative Chapter 8 source.

## 5. Chronology

The author explicitly states that Chapters 7, 8, and 9 all occur on the same day. This overrides older notes and manuscript variants that put Chapter 9 on the following day. Do not use a next-day opening.

## 6. Explicitly retired working assumptions

Do not use these as the primary explanation for Ira's acceptance:
- wanting to be useful;
- wanting social inclusion or validation;
- curiosity about Kuoh;
- accepting merely because someone friendly asks;
- generic altruism or heroic duty;
- fearlessness or sudden confidence.

Do not carry forward next-day chronology. Do not use the unsupported school-locker/Kenji/clamps/framing-shop setup as the bridge. Historical prose remains preserved as historical material; these are retired as operative planning assumptions.

## 7. Required review question

Before approving the opening, ask:

**Can the reader see that Ira accepts this particular errand because it is the first concrete opportunity to act on the resolve he formed in Chapter 8?**

If not, the handoff has failed, even if the errand is plausible and the prose is polished.
