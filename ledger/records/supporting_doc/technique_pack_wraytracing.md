# Wraytracing — Light Mechanics for Prose & Poetry

“Wraytracing” is a playful project name, not a formal academic term. Treat light as an event with a source, path, medium, surface, observer, and consequence, rather than a decorative color adjective.

## The ray model

For a significant lighting beat, ask:

1. **Source:** sun, moon, streetlamp, screen, flame, reflected surface?
2. **Path:** direct, blocked, scattered, reflected, refracted, diffused?
3. **Medium:** rain, dust, glass, water, smoke, fabric, leaves?
4. **Surface:** what receives the light, and what physical properties matter?
5. **Observer:** what can this viewpoint actually see, and what remains uncertain?
6. **Consequence:** what does the light reveal, conceal, distort, or reframe?

This is a compositional checklist, not a physics simulator. Use plausible optical relations unless the text deliberately presents an uncanny perception.

## Physical mechanics palette

Use these as a vocabulary of cause and effect, not as a demand to explain physics in every passage.

- **Direct propagation:** in ordinary scenes, light can be planned as rays traveling from a source. Ask what lies along the path and what blocks it.
- **Specular reflection:** a smooth surface can produce a directional glint or recognizable reflection; rough surfaces scatter light across many directions.
- **Refraction:** light changes direction as it passes between media, which can shift apparent position or distort an image.
- **Scattering:** particles, mist, dust, smoke, or rough surfaces redirect light; visible shafts require something in the path to scatter it toward the observer.
- **Absorption and color:** a surface's visible color depends on the light reaching it and the wavelengths it reflects or absorbs. Color descriptions should remain sensitive to the illumination.
- **Occlusion and shadow:** a body blocks some light. Edge sharpness depends on source size, distance, and geometry; a shadow's position must make sense relative to the source and surface unless it is intentionally uncanny.
- **Contrast and adaptation:** a bright source against darkness can hide nearby detail; after looking at a bright source, the observer's perception may temporarily change. Use the viewpoint's actual experience rather than treating the eye as a perfect camera.
- **Gravitational lensing:** massive objects can curve spacetime and distort paths of light. Near a black hole, the visible glow comes from surrounding matter such as a hot accretion disk, not from light escaping the black hole itself.

A physical model can support a lyrical passage without making the prose a science lecture. Pick the one interaction that carries the scene's purpose; do not list all the mechanics.

## Ledger evidence

- **Chapter 4:** stars, nebulae, an accretion disk, bent starlight, and the pale sphere translate cosmic scale into visible relations. These are Jonah's perceptions, not objective cosmology.
- **Chapter 4:** after cosmic abstraction, moonlight, car headlights, grass, and the road restore local scale and sensory certainty.
- **Chapter 6:** sunlight catches scratches in train glass; the academy figure's shadow appears too long, then seems ordinary on a second look. Preserve the shadow as an uncertain clue.
- **Chapter 1:** darkness removes visual access while sound and remembered room geometry maintain orientation.

## Application procedure

Identify the passage's purpose: orientation, beauty, concealment, scale, clue, mood, or transition. Name the source and actual interaction. Replace generic modifiers with one or two consequential effects. Keep the geometry and viewpoint legible. For an uncanny effect, distinguish what the character sees from what the story confirms.

## Failure modes

- Everything is golden, silver, or luminous.
- Too many optical effects stack up, making the scene impossible to picture.
- Scientific vocabulary is used without understanding the relation.
- The clue is explained before it has earned certainty.
- Light description pauses the scene without changing information or mood.

## Diagnostic test

Remove the color adjectives. Does light still do something? Then remove the light interaction. If nothing in spatial clarity, information, or emotion changes, the description may be decorative.

## Research connection

Use Jahn's [“Focalization”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-narrative/focalization/4292F5EB30ACFBB54BD1F8D4A920BED5) to keep visual information tied to a perspective. The light model is a practical craft synthesis, not a result directly validated by that source.
