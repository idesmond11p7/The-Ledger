# Embodied Prose and Perceptual Architecture

**Core Premise:** Words are not a canvas for painting static sensory inventories. Prose functions as a predictive simulation engine bound to an embodied human agent. Immersion breaks whenever an author or LLM halts narrative time to catalog surfaces, zooms obsessively into functioning tools, or flattens perception into RLHF cliché patterns.

---

## Part I: Cognitive Mechanics of Reading & Lived Continuity

1. **Event-Indexing & Spatial Asymmetry (Zwaan & Radvansky, 1995, 1998):**
   Readers automatically monitor Time, Causation, Motivation/Intentionality, and Protagonist status, but **do not reliably track spatial layout** unless space is fused directly to action, obstruction, or goal. Unindexed room/hallway inventories are discarded by working memory.
2. **Eye-Tracking Velocity Law (Mak & Willems, 2019, 2021, 2023):**
   - *Motor simulation* (bodily action, vector, impact) produces **shorter gaze durations** (faster reading speed).
   - *Perceptual description* and *mentalizing* produce **longer gaze durations** (slower reading speed).
   - **Rule:** Pacing is neuro-oculomotor. Never flood high-tempo beats with static visual adjectives or introspection; drive velocity through action-perception coupling.
3. **Predictive Processing & Attentional Filtering (Friston, 2010; Clark, 2013, 2016):**
   The brain suppresses expected ambient data and surfaces **prediction errors**—deviations from expectation, physical friction against an active goal, or interrupted routine. Objects earn page space when they snag, block, resist, or break a baseline.
4. **Transitive Fringe & Involuntary Memory (William James, 1890; Berntsen, 2009, 2015):**
   Lived continuity lives in *transitive states* (unfinished intentions, directional leanings, muscular readiness) rather than static nouns. Involuntary memories surface during diffuse attention and automated tasks via isolated sensory cues—not during high-load problem-solving.
5. **Temporal Carry-Over & Acoustic Latency:**
   A scene feels inhabited when physical states predate sentence one (a wet knot pulled tight at noon, a sock twisted on the walk from the bus) and background clocks run independently (pipes rushing before the character stops moving and notices them).

## Part II: Phenomenology of Action, Game Feel & Tactical Scale

1. **Heidegger’s Tool Analysis (1927) & Merleau-Ponty’s Body Schema:**
   - **Ready-to-hand (`Zuhandenheit`):** A functioning tool is transparent; attention projects downrange to the target/goal.
   - **Unready-to-hand (`Unzuhandenheit`):** Tool failure, jamming, or running dry breaks transparency and pulls attention back to the hardware.
   - **Present-at-hand (`Vorhandenheit`):** Detached inspection of physical properties.
   - **Rule:** Never trap active execution in `Vorhandenheit` macro-zoom. Keep functioning tools (weapons, pencils, satchels, maps) transparent; show mechanics only through impulse/recoil or genuine failure.
2. **Ecological Perception & Game Feel (Gibson, 1979; Swink, 2008):**
   - Render **affordances** (what a surface enables or blocks for the body), not neutral dimensions.
   - Use **optic flow / parallax** (foreground streaks fast; midground holds targets/people; far horizon shifts slowly).
   - Deliver **material causality**: impacts resolve against concrete, steel, cloth, or skin with distinct physical feedback.
3. **Camera Rig & Narratological Duration (Metz, 1977; Genette, 1972):**
   - **Metzian Rig:** Lock optical vantage strictly to the eye-line or over-the-shoulder envelope. Respect occlusion, distance resolution, and rack focus.
   - **Zero Genettian Pauses (`ST = 0, DT = n`):** Never freeze the world clock to describe a room or person. Gather all spatial/material data inside live `Scene (ST ≈ DT)` movement or `Rest & Arrest` (body halts while wind, rain, engines, and time continue).
4. **The Three Spatial Planes (Frostbite Scale Model):**
   - **Near Plane (0–2m, 10–15% budget):** Proprioceptive rig—recoil, cup warmth, coat weight, grit underfoot.
   - **Midground Plane (5–150m, ~70% active attention):** People, targets, cover, obstacles, kinetic interactions.
   - **Far Plane (200m+):** Macro-horizon anchor (academy spire, valley flats, streetlights, ridgelines) that prevents the scene from collapsing into a floating white box.
5. **High Dynamic Range (HDR) Audio (Strandberg / DICE):**
   Loud near-field transients (muzzle blasts, café door bells, passing trains, shouts) duck the ambient floor. The instant the local source cuts out, the acoustic aperture reopens and the wider environment rushes back in.

## Part III: LLM Prose Failure Taxonomy & Control Loop

### The Six RLHF / DPO Failure Modes to Purge
1. **Inflated Significance / Moralizing Coda:** Abstract thematic wrap-ups and *"It wasn't just X; it was Y"* negation pivots.
2. **Present-Participle Cascade:** Chaining `-ing` openers instead of linear causal syntax (`[Action A] -> [Consequence B] -> [Next Action C]`).
3. **Sensory Inventory Pause:** Static backdrop painting detached from affordance, goal, or prediction error.
4. **Broken-Tool Macro-Zoom:** Treating functioning equipment as an inspection catalog during active motion.
5. **Camera Drift:** Mind-reading distant characters, describing the protagonist's own face without a mirror, or violating physical occlusion.
6. **Melodramatic Emotion Naming:** Substituting abstract labels (*"a wave of terror washed over him"*) for physical/transitive states.

### Three-Stage Cognitive Control Loop (for Prompting & Auditing Writer)
- **Stage 1 — Architectural Telemetry (Before Drafting):** Define Focalization Rig & occlusion, Three Spatial Planes (Near/Mid/Far), Active Goal & Tool State, Pre-Existing Clock/Latency, and HDR Audio Ducking points.
- **Stage 2 — Execution Constraints:** Zero Genettian pauses; Mak & Willems velocity calibration (motor verbs for high tempo, diffuse/transitive beats for low tempo); ban negation pivots and melodramatic emotion labels.
- **Stage 3 — Five-Point Adversarial Post-Audit:**
  1. *Inventory Audit:* Cut unindexed multi-adjective visual padding.
  2. *Heidegger Audit:* Compress functioning tool mechanics to impulse/feedback; push attention downrange.
  3. *Gaze Velocity Audit:* Break `-ing` chains and detached metaphors in fast beats.
  4. *Camera-Occlusion Audit:* Cut anything invisible from the character's actual eye-line/distance.
  5. *Latency & Acoustic Audit:* Verify pre-existing clocks and HDR acoustic ducking/recovery.

## Part IV: Circulator Calibration Notes (Operational Guardrails)

1. **Two-Register Firewall:** Never paste academic jargon (`Genettian Pause`, `Zuhandenheit`, `Mak & Willems`, `Metzian Rig`, `WRayTracing`) directly into Writer prose prompts. Translate every rule into plain, skull-locked physical execution cues so the Writer does not leak theory or perform the mechanics self-consciously.
2. **Nuance on Emotion Labels (Barrett / Lazarus Balance):** Ban melodramatic emotional telling (*"terror washed over him"*), but do not enforce an unnatural robotic ban on plain cognitive appraisal words (*annoyed, relieved, uneasy*) when they fit Ira/Jonah's dry, analytical first-person voice.
3. **Ballistic Material Check (Benchmark Errata):** In the River Valley Berm benchmark specimen below, note one minor game-trope artifact (`"two sparks snapped off the runner's chest plate"`); modern ceramic/Kevlar armor produces dust puffs or torn fabric rather than steel sparks. Preserve the structure and velocity, keep material physics strictly grounded.

## Part V: Verified Benchmark Specimens (Non-Canon)

### Specimen A — Low-Tempo Predictive Processing & Latency (The Stairwell)
> The left shoe wouldn’t come off until he sat down on the lowest stair and dug a thumbnail into the wet knot he’d pulled too tight at noon. His coat was still on, heavy across his shoulders, holding the cold of the street.
>
> Above him, through the floorboards, water was running through the pipes—not a sudden sound, just the hollow rush that had probably been going since he turned the latch, only now that he’d stopped moving, it filled the stairwell.
>
> He worked the lace loose, pulled his heel free with a small drag of damp cotton, and set his foot down on the linoleum. The sock had twisted during the walk from the bus; the seam was pressed into the top of his toes, sore and familiar. He knew he ought to get up, turn the valve on the kitchen radiator before the system air-locked again, and find something to eat, but the quiet in the stairwell had a dead weight to it that made sitting there easy.
>
> Outside, a car door slammed—a flat, clean crack against the brick across the street. The starter turned three times, caught on a dry cough, and the noise pulled away down the avenue. When the sound thinned out into the traffic three blocks over, nothing replaced it except the refrigerator shivering behind the kitchen wall, running the same long, dull cycle that never quite shut off.

### Specimen B — High-Tempo Three-Plane Scale, Tool Transparency & HDR Audio (The River Valley Berm)
> The berm gave him three feet of churned mud and crushed concrete to work with. He drove his chest into the slope, the bipod digging in, the world opening up across the river valley: two hundred meters of ruined industrial flats, crossed by burning fuel trucks and the steady, flat-line arc of red tracers stitching the overcast sky.
>
> Three silhouettes broke from the blown-out corner of the rail depot at sixty yards, sprinting low for a rusted shipping container.
>
> He didn't think about the rifle; the glass of the optic simply found the lead runner’s chest. He pressed the trigger twice. Two heavy punches into his right shoulder—crack-crack—and downrange, two sparks snapped off the runner’s chest plate. The man didn't stumble; he folded violently at the waist and hit the wet gravel face-first.
>
> The crosshair whipped right without pause, cutting across the open mud to the second man.
>
> The second target saw the drop, cut his stride to slide behind a concrete culvert, but his shoulder was still exposed. The rifle barked three times, the muzzle blast blowing a circle of dried grass flat beneath the barrel. The rounds chewed into the lip of the concrete—gray plumes of pulverized stone spattering into the man’s helmet—and he rolled backward into the ditch, out of sight.
>
> The third target made the steel container. His rifle barrel poked around the corrugated edge, flashing white.
>
> Rounds whipped over the berm—that vicious, tearing snap-snap-snap of supersonic lead passing inches overhead, snapping the air and showering his back with dirt clods. He didn't drop his head. He dropped the crosshair six inches to the left of the container's corner, anticipating the man leaning out to re-engage, and let three rounds go through the thin sheet metal.
>
> A dull, wet thud answered through the steel. A rifle clattered against the gravel, and the barrel slipped from the edge.
>
> He let off the trigger.
>
> The immediate roar of his own muzzle died, and instantly the wider valley rushed back into his ears: the low, rolling rumble of armor grinding over asphalt three streets over, the heavy concussive thuds of mortar fire walking up the ridgeline behind him, and the smell of wet dirt mixed with burning diesel blowing straight down the river.
