# Research Receipt — Psycholinguistics, Cadence & Typography

## Reader prediction

**Wong, Reichle & Veldre, “Prediction in reading: A review of predictability effects, their theoretical implications, and beyond,” Psychonomic Bulletin & Review (online 2024; volume 2025).**  
https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02588-z

This review surveys predictability research and stresses that effects require careful interpretation across measures and processing stages. Prediction is a useful craft lens, not a deterministic way to control readers.

**Ryskin and colleagues, “Prediction during language comprehension: what is next?” (2023).**  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11614350/

The review describes evidence that readers and listeners use context to predict upcoming input while noting unresolved questions about the mechanisms.

## Inference and coherence

**Trabasso & Suh, “Understanding text: Achieving explanatory coherence through on-line inferences and mental operations in working memory,” Discourse Processes (1993).**  
https://doi.org/10.1080/01638539309544827

The abstract reports work on causal inferences and describes maintaining, retrieving, elaborating, and explaining information during comprehension. This supports checking whether a reader can connect events and retrieve relevant cues.

**Márta Horváth, “Causal expectations: Suspense and surprise while reading Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone” (2023).**  
https://doi.org/10.14232/ncognito/2023.2.35-46

The article discusses causal expectations as part of suspense and surprise. Surprise alone does not guarantee a satisfying effect.

## Prose rhythm

**Alan Roberts, “Rhythm in Prose and the Serial Correlation of Sentence Lengths: a Joyce Cary Case Study,” Literary and Linguistic Computing (1996).**  
https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article-abstract/11/1/33/969582

The study examines sentence-length patterns across spans of prose, supporting analysis of sequences rather than isolated sentences. It does not establish one ideal rhythm.

**“Reading Poetry and Prose: Eye Movements and Acoustic Evidence,” Discourse Processes (2022).**  
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2021.2015188

The study reports genre-related differences in oral reading, including pauses and rhythmic prominence in the studied poetry. Do not convert this into a rule that all poetry must be slow or all prose fast.

## Typography

**Kate Scott & Rebecca Jackson, “When EVERYTHING STANDS OUT, Nothing Does: Typography, expectations and procedures” (2020).**  
https://benjamins.com/catalog/ftl.8.06sco

Typography can guide interpretation through capitalization, boldface, and underlining, but its effect depends on the rest of the text and reader expectations.

**Daniel Matore, The Graphics of Verse (Oxford University Press, 2023).**  
https://academic.oup.com/book/55287

The book overview discusses spacing, indentation, alignment, and typeface as expressive resources in experimental poetry.

## Practical limits

Use research to form testable craft questions, not to dress preferences in neuroscience language. Distinguish observed study findings from interpretations of the Ledger chapters. When full text is unavailable, do not invent page-specific claims.
