# Research Receipt — Focalization, Gaps, Montage & Narrative Structure

This synthesis uses publisher pages, encyclopedia entries, and available abstracts. It does not claim full-text access to every source.

## Focalization

**Manfred Jahn, “Focalization,” The Cambridge Companion to Narrative (2007).**  
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-narrative/focalization/4292F5EB30ACFBB54BD1F8D4A920BED5

Focalization filters narrative information through a perspective, restricting access to what is available and relevant. This supports treating the “camera” as a control over knowledge, attention, and interpretation, not merely visual angles.

**Daniel P. Gunn, “Free Indirect Discourse,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Literature (2019).**  
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61883/chapter-abstract/547772880

Free indirect discourse blends narratorial language with a character's idiom and subjectivity without an explicit thought-report frame. It can produce sympathy or irony; it is not a synonym for all interiority.

## Narrative gaps and inference

**H. Porter Abbott, “How do we read what isn’t there to be Read? Shadow Stories and Permanent Gaps,” Oxford Handbook of Cognitive Literary Studies (2015).**  
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28025/chapter-abstract/211842613

The abstract distinguishes gaps that invite possible “shadow stories” from permanent gaps that need not resolve into a definite missing event. A gap does not always encode one hidden truth.

**Neil Cohn, “Being explicit about the implicit: inference generating techniques in visual narrative,” Language and Cognition (2019).**  
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-and-cognition/article/being-explicit-about-the-implicit-inference-generating-techniques-in-visual-narrative/AEBDBD7A09A3892D860463AB57588112

The paper examines how specific omissions and substitutions prompt inference in visual sequences. By analogy, omission effects depend on the material omitted and surrounding structure, not on absence alone.

## Montage and narrative operations

**Kerstin Barndt, “Montage in Literature,” Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism.**  
https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/overview/montage

Literary montage joins heterogeneous discourses or fragments, including documents, slogans, songs, and conflicting registers. It is more specific than merely placing two descriptions next to each other.

**“Narrative structure in the mind: Translating Genette’s narrative discourse theory into a cognitive system” (2019).**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1389041719304656

The paper represents operations such as scene, pause, ellipsis, iterative narration, focalization, and narrative order. These are useful analytical operations, not a universal recipe for good prose.
