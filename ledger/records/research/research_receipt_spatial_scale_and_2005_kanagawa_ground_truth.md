# Research Receipt — Spatial Scale Architecture & 2005 Kanagawa/Tokyo Ground Truth

## 1. Verified Spatial Scale Mechanics (Anti-Sightseeing Execution)

A space feels largest when the eye cannot finish measuring it—but per Zwaan et al. (1998), Mak & Willems (2019/2021), and Predictive Processing (Friston/Clark), raw architectural sightseeing tours fail and stall gaze velocity. Every spatial principle below must be rendered strictly through **access friction, thermal/acoustic shifts, and body posture** inside live First-Person eye-line action:

- ***Oku* (奥 — Fumihiko Maki) & *Miegakure* (Hide-and-Reveal):** Layered inward depth and partial occlusion. Never list thresholds as a tour ("stone gate -> courtyard -> glass bridge"). Reveal depth through operational barriers and human traffic: an examinee badge clearing only one wing, glass double doors locked behind an IC reader that ignores paper slips, upper corridors bending out of sight where uniformed students disappear while examinees are funneled left.
- **Environmental Mystery (Arthur E. Stamps III, 2007 — Corrected):**
  - The strongest empirical driver of environmental mystery is **low light / darkness** (`r = -.63`), not generic "light contrast."
  - The second largest driver is **combined visual + locomotive occlusion** (`d = .62`—blocking both sightline and physical passage, such as a frosted security door or shadowed stairwell turn). Blocking movement alone (`d = .15`) or raw depth of view (`d = .11`) has minimal effect.
- **Compression & Release (Frank Lloyd Wright) & Prospect-Refuge (Jay Appleton, 1975):**
  - Never state ceiling height as static geometry. Render compression via cramped shoulder clearance, dry heater air, chalk/paper odor, and tight acoustic bounce (Room 301). Render release via sudden temperature drop, cold air hitting a warm collar, footsteps ringing wide, and floor space opening out under glass on an elevated colonnade.

## 2. Verified 2005 Kanagawa / Tokyo Ground Truth

- **Early-March 2005 Astronomy & Weather (NAOJ / JMA):**
  - Sunrise: ~6:11 AM (Mar 1) to ~5:52 AM (Mar 15).
  - Sunset: ~5:36 PM (Mar 1) to ~5:48 PM (Mar 15).
  - Early March 2005 in Kanto was abnormally cold (~3.9°C mean vs. 5.7°C normal; March 4, 2005 brought 2 cm of snow to Tokyo/Yokohama with ~3.6–4.1°C highs).
  - **Chronology Rule:** Rain cannot fake dusk at midday. Streetlights turning on at Ira's apartment require his exam day to run past ~3:00–3:15 PM, putting him home around 5:15–5:35 PM.
- **March High School Exam Calendar (Corrected):**
  - Standard Japanese private and Kanagawa/Tokyo public general entrance exams finish in **February**.
  - An early-March exam at Soma Academy MUST explicitly be a **returnee/transfer admission track (`帰国生編入試験` / `転・編入試験`)** or special March late-round selection—which aligns directly with Ira's *Overseas Transfer / Independent Curriculum* dossier and naturally accounts for afternoon Science/applied or written/interview modules pushing dismissal past 3:00 PM.
- **2005 Kanagawa Transit Seam (Corrected — Half-IC, Half-Magnetic):**
  - Do NOT treat the outer city as purely paper-ticketed. **JR East** launched contactless FeliCa **Suica** in November 2001 across Tokyo/Kanagawa and enabled Suica retail e-money in March 2004.
  - **Private railways & municipal subways** (Odakyu, Tokyu, Keikyu, Sotetsu, Yokohama Municipal Subway) did NOT get contactless IC (PASMO) until March 18, 2007. In March 2005, non-JR lines ran strictly on **paper magnetic tickets and Passnet magnetic prepaid cards** (plus magnetic Common Bus Cards).
  - **Prose Application:** Use the real 2005 seam—contactless beep on JR gates, then mechanical slot-feed paper/Passnet ticket on the private line to/from Soma Station.
- **Verified 2005 Campus Tech (Soma Academy):**
